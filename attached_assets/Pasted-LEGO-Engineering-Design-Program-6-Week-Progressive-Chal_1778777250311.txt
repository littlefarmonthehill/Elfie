LEGO Engineering & Design Program

6-Week Progressive Challenge Series

Ages 7–14 | Grades 2–8

This program is designed to grow with students — starting with exploration and creativity in younger grades, then advancing into engineering, systems thinking, collaboration, and design iteration for older students.

⸻

Week 1 — Structure & Stability

Core Concepts

* Balance
* Weight distribution
* Structural integrity
* Buoyancy

⸻

Tower Building

Challenge: Build the tallest freestanding tower possible.

Grades 2–3

* Free build exploration
* Discover how height affects stability
* Focus on creativity and experimentation

Grades 4–5

* Tower must:
    * Stand for 30 seconds
    * Hold a small weight on top

Grades 6–8

* Build:
    * Tallest tower
    * Supporting the most weight
* Students document:
    * Why the design worked
    * Failure points and improvements

⸻

Bridge Building

Challenge: Build the longest bridge that supports a heavy book.

Grades 2–3

* Span a gap between two tables
* Use any pieces desired

Grades 4–5

* Bridge must:
    * Span a set distance
    * Hold a specific weight

Grades 6–8

* Optimize for:
    * Longest span
    * Maximum load capacity
* Analyze where structural failure occurs

⸻

Buoyancy

Challenge: Build the largest LEGO creation that floats for 15 seconds.

Grades 2–3

* Free floating experiment
* Discovery-focused testing

Grades 4–5

* Must:
    * Float successfully
    * Carry small cargo

Grades 6–8

* Maximize cargo capacity
* Predict weight limits before testing

⸻

Week 2 — Force & Motion

Core Concepts

* Speed
* Friction
* Gravity
* Aerodynamics
* Motion systems

⸻

Race Cars

Challenge: Build and race a LEGO car.

Grades 2–3

* Simple downhill race
* Fastest car wins

Grades 4–5

* Must include:
    * Functional axle system
* Students test:
    * Wheel size
    * Weight placement

Grades 6–8

* Optimize using:
    * Gear ratios
    * Weight distribution
    * Aerodynamics
* Iterative redesign between races

⸻

Marble Run

Challenge: Guide a marble from top to bottom.

Grades 2–3

* Simple ramp system

Grades 4–5

* Include:
    * Multiple levels
    * Two direction changes

Grades 6–8

* Longest or most controlled run
* Students redesign for:
    * Speed
    * Slow-motion effects
    * Reliability

⸻

Aircraft

Challenge: Build something that moves through the air.

Grades 2–3

* Gliders or flying creatures
* Test travel distance

Grades 4–5

* Must travel a minimum distance
* Students adjust:
    * Shape
    * Weight

Grades 6–8

* Optimize for:
    * Distance
    * Hang time
* Apply basic aerodynamics concepts

⸻

Week 3 — Systems & Connections

Core Concepts

* Urban planning
* Collaboration
* Infrastructure
* Accessibility
* Systems thinking

⸻

City of the Future

Challenge: Design an ideal connected city.

Grades 2–3

* Build:
    * Houses
    * Roads
    * One landmark

Grades 4–5

* Include:
    * Transportation system
    * Three connected zones

Grades 6–8

* Full city plan with:
    * Infrastructure
    * Sustainability feature
    * Organized zoning
* Students present design reasoning

⸻

Collaboration Challenge

Challenge: Separate builds must connect into one system.

Grades 2–3

* Individual buildings combine into a street

Grades 4–5

* Team-built districts connect utilities and roads

Grades 6–8

* Large-scale city collaboration
* Teams negotiate:
    * Shared resources
    * Connections
    * Consistent design language

⸻

Playground Design

Challenge: Build a functional playground.

Grades 2–3

* Dream playground free build

Grades 4–5

* Must include:
    * Three connected features
    * Moving/interacting components

Grades 6–8

* Design for a specific audience:
    * Toddlers
    * Accessibility needs
    * Older children
* Justify all design choices

⸻

Week 4 — Design & Iteration

Core Concepts

* Problem solving
* Engineering redesign
* Iteration
* Chain reactions

⸻

Rube Goldberg Machine

Challenge: Create a chain reaction machine.

Grades 2–3

* Two-step reaction

Grades 4–5

* Four-step sequence
* Plan sequence before building

Grades 6–8

* Six+ steps using multiple force types
* Students troubleshoot failures

⸻

Repair or Replace

Challenge: Fix or redesign a broken build.

Grades 2–3

* Repair simple structure

Grades 4–5

* Repair and improve functionality

Grades 6–8

* Full redesign:
    * Diagnose failures
    * Rebuild from scratch
    * Justify improvements

⸻

Tool Design

Challenge: Build something useful.

Grades 2–3

* Create any useful object

Grades 4–5

* Must:
    * Use every piece
    * Solve a defined problem

Grades 6–8

* Present and pitch:
    * Problem solved
    * Functionality
    * Demonstration

⸻

Week 5 — Space & Planning

Core Concepts

* Spatial reasoning
* Strategy
* Scale
* Planning systems

⸻

Board Game Design

Challenge: Build a playable LEGO board game.

Grades 2–3

* Simple path-based game

Grades 4–5

* Include:
    * Obstacles
    * Scoring system
    * Custom pieces

Grades 6–8

* Full strategy game with:
    * Unique rules
    * Roles
    * Competitive testing

⸻

Maze or Labyrinth

Challenge: Build a navigable maze.

Grades 2–3

* Basic start-to-finish maze

Grades 4–5

* Timed challenge maze
* Build for difficulty

Grades 6–8

* Multi-level maze with:
    * Dead ends
    * Traps
    * Pre-planned layouts

⸻

Micro-Scale City

Challenge: Build a tiny-scale world.

Grades 2–3

* Mini home or school

Grades 4–5

* Mini town with:
    * Roads
    * Five buildings

Grades 6–8

* Planned micro-city including:
    * Districts
    * Infrastructure
    * Green spaces

⸻

Week 6 — Resources & Constraints

Core Concepts

* Resourcefulness
* Efficiency
* Creative limitations
* Optimization

⸻

Random Brick Challenge

Challenge: Solve a problem using random bricks.

Grades 2–3

* Open creativity challenge

Grades 4–5

* Functional build using all pieces

Grades 6–8

* Solve a defined engineering problem
* Present solution before and after building

⸻

30 Pieces Challenge

Challenge: Build using exactly 30 pieces.

Grades 2–3

* Most creative design possible

Grades 4–5

* Must:
    * Be functional
    * Be structurally sound

Grades 6–8

* Specific engineering prompt:
    * Bridge
    * Shelter
    * Vehicle
* Most effective solution wins

⸻

2x2 Bricks Only

Challenge: Build using only 2x2 bricks.

Grades 2–3

* Explore shapes and stacking

Grades 4–5

* Build recognizable structures
* Stability and height judged

Grades 6–8

* Engineering optimization challenge:
    * Strongest arch
    * Tallest tower
    * Widest bridge
* Students compare and explain outcomes

⸻

Program Outcomes

By the end of the 6 weeks, students will have practiced:

* Engineering thinking
* Iterative design
* Collaboration
* Communication
* Spatial reasoning
* Problem-solving
* Creativity under constraints
* Real-world STEM applications

The progression intentionally moves from:

1. Building structures
2. Understanding motion
3. Creating systems
4. Improving designs
5. Planning environments
6. Working within constraints
