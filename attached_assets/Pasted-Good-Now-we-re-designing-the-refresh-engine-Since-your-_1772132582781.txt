Good. Now we’re designing the refresh engine.

Since your objective is max margin + automation, your tiers should not be based on LEGO “category names” alone.

They should be based on:
	•	Margin potential
	•	Market volatility
	•	Revenue contribution
	•	API efficiency

But we can use part categories as a shortcut layer.

Below is a clean, scalable tier structure that works extremely well at your size (~20k lots).

⸻

🥇 Tier 1 — High Volatility / High Margin (Daily Cycle)

Refresh: Daily

These are your profit drivers.

Categories:
	•	Minifigures (complete)
	•	Minifigure heads / torsos / legs
	•	Minifigure accessories (weapons, animals)
	•	Printed parts
	•	Licensed/theme-specific parts (Star Wars, Marvel, etc.)
	•	Rare colors (sand red, dark turquoise, old brown, etc.)
	•	Retired / discontinued parts

Why?
	•	Supply shifts quickly
	•	Buyers are less price-sensitive
	•	Low availability = premium opportunity

This tier is usually ~10–20% of lots but drives a large portion of margin.

⸻

🥈 Tier 2 — Strong Demand Structural Parts (Every 2–3 Days)

Refresh: Every 2–3 days (rotating batches)

Categories:
	•	Slopes
	•	Brackets
	•	Modified plates
	•	Tiles with clips
	•	Hinges
	•	Technic connectors
	•	Popular MOC structural pieces

Why?
	•	Good velocity
	•	Medium volatility
	•	Margin sensitive to availability changes

⸻

🥉 Tier 3 — Core Commodity Bricks & Plates (Weekly)

Refresh: Weekly

Categories:
	•	Basic bricks (2x4, 2x2, 1x4, etc.)
	•	Basic plates
	•	Standard tiles
	•	Common Technic beams
	•	Common colors (black, white, red, blue, LBG, DBG)

Why?
	•	Extremely stable pricing
	•	High competition
	•	Thin margins
	•	Mostly cart-fillers

No reason to burn daily API calls here.

⸻

🧊 Tier 4 — Deep Inventory / Low Velocity (Monthly or Event-Driven)

Refresh: Monthly OR when triggered

Categories:
	•	Parts with zero sales in 6+ months
	•	Extremely high market supply
	•	Bulk color variants nobody hunts for
	•	Oddball legacy parts

These don’t justify frequent refreshes.

⸻

🔥 Bonus Layer: Quantity-Based Overrides

Regardless of category:

If your stock ≤ 5 units
→ promote to Tier 1 temporarily.

If your stock ≥ 500 units
→ demote to Tier 3.

Scarcity inside your store matters too.

⸻

🧠 Even Better: Revenue-Based Overlay

After 30 days of data, classify:
	•	Top 20% revenue lots → Tier 1 or 2 automatically
	•	Bottom 30% → Tier 3 or 4

This keeps system aligned with profit reality.

⸻

📊 Practical Daily API Allocation Example

If you have ~20k lots:
	•	Tier 1: 3,000 lots (daily)
	•	Tier 2: 6,000 lots (rotate 1/3 every day = 2,000/day)
	•	Tier 3: 8,000 lots (1/7 weekly rotation ≈ 1,150/day)
	•	Tier 4: 3,000 lots (monthly ≈ 100/day)

Total daily calls ≈ 4,250
Safe under 5,000.

⸻

🧭 Why This Works for Max Margin

You:
	•	Extract premium where volatility exists
	•	Avoid over-refreshing commodities
	•	Let stable parts float
	•	Focus compute on money

Automation stays intact.
Margins increase without micromanaging.
