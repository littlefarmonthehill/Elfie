To build a suggested price generator, the goal is to estimate a price that balances:
	•	Historical willingness to pay (sold prices)
	•	Current competition (lowest listing)
	•	Demand strength
	•	Supply scarcity

Then you add your 10% store premium on top.

Below is a practical pricing model used in many marketplaces, adapted to the metrics you already track.

⸻

Suggested Price Generator

1. Base Market Price

First calculate a balanced market reference price using historical and current market signals.

Calculation

base_market_price =
    (market_avg_sold_price * 0.5) +
    (market_lowest_price * 0.3) +
    (market_highest_sold_price * 0.2)

Why this works
	•	Average sold price anchors the real market value
	•	Lowest listing keeps you competitive
	•	Highest sold price allows upward potential

⸻

Demand Adjustment

If demand is strong, prices can rise.

demand_adjustment =
    1 + (demand_velocity * 0.25)

Example

demand_velocity = 0.40
adjustment = 1 + (0.40 * 0.25)
adjustment = 1.10

Meaning 10% demand premium.

⸻

Scarcity Adjustment

Low supply allows a small price increase.

scarcity_adjustment =
    1 + (market_scarcity_index * supply_weight)

Example simplified:

scarcity_adjustment =
    1 + (1 / market_supply * 50)

This prevents overreacting to supply spikes.

⸻

Raw Suggested Price

raw_suggested_price =
    base_market_price *
    demand_adjustment *
    scarcity_adjustment


⸻

Competitive Adjustment (Optional but recommended)

Prevent pricing too far above the market.

competitive_price =
    min(raw_suggested_price, market_lowest_price * 1.15)

Meaning you won’t price more than 15% above the lowest listing unless demand is extremely strong.

⸻

Final Suggested Price (with Store Premium)

You mentioned your 10% premium store fee.

suggested_price =
    competitive_price * 1.10


⸻

Final Formula (Simplified)

Your pricing engine could implement something like:

suggested_price =
(
    (
        (market_avg_sold_price * 0.5) +
        (market_lowest_price * 0.3) +
        (market_highest_sold_price * 0.2)
    )
    *
    (1 + demand_velocity * 0.25)
)
* 1.10


⸻

Example

Market data

avg sold = $0.80
highest sold = $1.10
lowest listing = $0.70
demand velocity = 0.45

Step 1 — base price

base =
(0.80 * 0.5) +
(0.70 * 0.3) +
(1.10 * 0.2)

base = 0.83

Step 2 — demand adjustment

1 + (0.45 * 0.25) = 1.11

raw price = 0.83 * 1.11 = 0.92

Step 3 — store premium

suggested_price = 0.92 * 1.10 = $1.01

Final suggestion:

$1.01

⸻

Simple Implementation Version (Good for v1)

You could start with a much simpler but effective version:

suggested_price =
(
    (market_avg_sold_price * 0.6) +
    (market_lowest_price * 0.4)
)
* 1.10

This works surprisingly well for LEGO part marketplaces.

⸻

One Important Trick LEGO Repricers Use

Advanced LEGO repricers also add:

Minimum price floor

price_floor =
market_lowest_price * 0.95

Then:

suggested_price =
max(suggested_price, price_floor)

This prevents pricing too far below the market.
