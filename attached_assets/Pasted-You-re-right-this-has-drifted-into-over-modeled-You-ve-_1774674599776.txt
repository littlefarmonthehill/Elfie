You’re right—this has drifted into “over-modeled.” You’ve essentially got two layers doing similar work:
	•	Strategy weights (user intent)
	•	Score calculation (system math)

And they’re overlapping enough that it’s creating friction instead of clarity.

Let’s simplify this in a way that matches how humans actually think about pricing decisions.

⸻

🔧 Core Insight

Users don’t think in formulas. They think in tradeoffs:

“Do I want to sell fast, or make more money?”

So instead of weighting 4 semi-overlapping metrics, you want to collapse this into clear strategic intent.

⸻

✅ Step 1: Collapse Your Dimensions

Right now you have:
	•	Ceiling
	•	Velocity (Demand)
	•	Scarcity (Rarity)
	•	Undercut (Competition)

These reduce cleanly into 3 real drivers:

1. 💰 Profit (Ceiling + Scarcity)

“How high can I push price?”

2. ⚡ Speed (Velocity / Demand)

“How fast will this sell?”

3. 🥊 Competition (Undercut pressure)

“How aggressive do I need to be vs others?”

👉 That’s it. Everything maps cleanly into those 3.

⸻

❌ Fix the Undercut Problem

You spotted this correctly:

Undercut should not be additive.

Instead:
	•	Treat Competition as a pressure, not a benefit
	•	It should lower price OR increase urgency, not boost score

So instead of:

+ undercut

Think:

competition → pushes toward lower price or faster selling


⸻

🎯 Step 2: Replace Weights with Intent (MUCH simpler UX)

Option A (Best): 3-Slider Tradeoff Model

Give users 3 sliders that must total 100:
	•	Profit
	•	Speed
	•	Competition Awareness

Example presets:
	•	Max Profit → 70 / 20 / 10
	•	Balanced → 33 / 33 / 33
	•	Clear Inventory Fast → 20 / 70 / 10
	•	Highly Competitive Market → 30 / 30 / 40

👉 This keeps your math intact but makes UX intuitive.

⸻

Option B: Single Dial (Even Cleaner)

A single slider:

[ Max Profit ◀──────────────▶ Max Speed ]

Then:
	•	Left = prioritize ceiling & scarcity
	•	Right = prioritize velocity & undercut

Competition becomes automatic, not user-controlled.

👉 This is extremely powerful for simplicity.

⸻

Option C: Preset Strategies (My favorite for your product)

Let users pick a mindset instead of building one.

Example:
	•	🟢 Premium Seller
	•	High ceiling
	•	Low undercut
	•	Ignores competition unless extreme
	•	🔵 Balanced Seller
	•	Moderate everything
	•	🟡 Volume Seller
	•	High velocity
	•	Moderate undercut
	•	🔴 Aggressive Liquidator
	•	Strong undercut
	•	Max velocity
	•	Ignores ceiling

Then optionally:
👉 “Customize” → opens sliders

⸻

🧠 Step 3: Kill the “Score” (or hide it)

Right now:

Score = (ceiling × 40%) + …

This is internal logic, not user value.

Instead:

Rank items by:
	•	“Best Profit Opportunity”
	•	“Fastest Sellers”
	•	“Most Competitive Risk”

👉 Let users sort by meaningful labels, not math.

⸻

🔁 Suggested New Model (Clean + Scalable)

User Input:

Choose one:

Simple Mode:
	•	Preset strategy (1 click)

Advanced Mode:
	•	3 sliders: Profit / Speed / Competition (must total 100)

⸻

System Logic (hidden):

You still compute:
	•	ceiling
	•	velocity
	•	scarcity
	•	undercut

But map them like:

profit_score = ceiling + scarcity
speed_score = velocity
competition_score = undercut pressure

Then apply weights behind the scenes.

⸻

🎨 UX Upgrade Idea (Big Win)

Replace your radar chart with:

A Triangle (3 forces)

        Profit
         /\
        /  \
       /    \
Speed ------- Competition

Users drag a point inside.

👉 This visually teaches tradeoffs instantly.

⸻

⚡ Final Recommendation

If you want the cleanest, most scalable system:

Do this:
	1.	Collapse to 3 dimensions
	2.	Offer 4 presets
	3.	Add optional 3-slider customization
	4.	Remove visible “score math”
	5.	Treat competition as directional pressure, not additive

⸻

	•	don’t want to think like analysts

So the real goal isn’t:

“Let them tune everything”

It’s:

“Help them feel like the system thinks like they do”
