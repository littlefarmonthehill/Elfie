The idea is to detect high-demand / low-supply parts where buyers will pay above market floor because they just need the part to complete a set.

⸻

Premium Opportunity Score

What it measures

Identifies parts where you should intentionally price above the lowest listing because:
	•	demand is strong
	•	supply is limited
	•	buyers prioritize availability over price

These are often:
	•	rare colors
	•	old mold variations
	•	small quantities needed for MOCs or sets

⸻

Premium Opportunity Score Calculation

premium_opportunity_score =
(demand_velocity * 0.6) +
(market_scarcity_index * 0.4)

Where:

demand_velocity = market_qty_sold / market_qty_supply
market_scarcity_index = 1 / market_qty_supply


⸻

Interpretation

Score	Strategy
Low	Price competitively
Medium	Match market
High	Add premium above market

Example thresholds:

0.00 – 0.20  → Competitive pricing
0.20 – 0.40  → Match market
0.40+        → Premium pricing


⸻

Premium Pricing Adjustment

If the premium opportunity score is high, increase price relative to the lowest listing.

Example rule:

premium_multiplier =
1 + (premium_opportunity_score * 0.5)

Example:

premium_opportunity_score = 0.50
premium_multiplier = 1.25

Meaning:

Price 25% above market floor.

⸻

Premium Suggested Price

Combine it with the earlier suggested price model.

premium_price =
suggested_price * premium_multiplier

Then apply your store fee.

final_price =
premium_price * 1.10


⸻

Example

Market data:

lowest price = $0.60
avg sold = $0.75
highest sold = $1.10
sold qty = 450
supply qty = 500

Demand:

demand_velocity = 450 / 500 = 0.90

Scarcity:

market_scarcity_index = 1 / 500 = 0.002

Premium score:

premium_score =
(0.90 * 0.6) + (0.002 * 0.4)

premium_score ≈ 0.54

Multiplier:

1 + (0.54 * 0.5) = 1.27

If suggested price was:

$0.85

Premium price:

0.85 * 1.27 = $1.08

Store fee:

1.08 * 1.10 = $1.19

Final suggested price:

$1.19

Even though the lowest listing is $0.60.

This happens frequently with LEGO parts.

Real Behavior on BrickLink

Large sellers rely on the fact that:
	•	buyers often buy many parts from one store
	•	they prefer fewer stores
	•	they prioritize inventory availability

So they will pay more if your store has the parts they need.
